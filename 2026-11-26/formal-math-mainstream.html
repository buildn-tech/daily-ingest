OpenAI ships math preprints with Lean formalizations

strong signalpreprintopenai/math
  • github.com/openai/math · committed Nov 25, 2026
  • "The matrix multiplication exponent is nine fourths" (ω ≤ 9/4)
  • "Superpolynomial graph diameter of simple polytopes" — Hirsch conjecture is false
  • Both landed as paper.pdf + Lean formalizations in one commit

What it is. A serious ω bound (9/4, improving on the long-standing 2.37× results) and a disproof of the polynomial Hirsch conjecture, published as GitHub preprints whose commit message says "manuscripts and Lean formalizations" — the checker runs, so the claim is load-bearing from day one.

Why it matters now. Five days ago GRH was a one-off anomaly; today the same release shape came from a second lab with two theorems at once. The verification-first release is becoming a lab norm, and it changes who can verify what: a machine, not a seminar.

Buildn / client use. The transferable pattern is artifact-with-checker as the deliverable: a report nobody can re-verify is marketing; a report with a runnable check is a product. Worth mirroring in Buildn OS artifacts — every claim card should carry a checkable piece.

  • Two labs, same release shape
  • Checker ships with the claim
  • Community verifies machines, not prose
Preprints, vendor-authored. The ω = 9/4 result has ~590 HN comments of scrutiny; treat the headline as "claimed and machine-checked", not "community-accepted".

Anthropic's compact GRH proof: 154× shorter, independently checked

material updateresearchanthropic
  • github.com/lsernau/grh-lean · commit 72fead1, Nov 25, 2026
  • grh/ readable: ~300k lines · grh-compact: ~76k lines
  • Alpöge original: ~17M lines (Nov 20) — this is 154× shorter
  • MIT verification files: math.mit.edu/~drew/shortgrh/ (two machines)
  • Mathlib-pinned, only propext / Classical.choice / Quot.sound

What it is. Luke Sernau (Anthropic) and Claude rewrote the 17-million-line GRH Lean proof into a readable (~300k) and compact (~76k) edition proving the same five theorems, with independent verification by Andrew Sutherland at MIT. No mathematician has read it end to end; no paper accompanies it.

What's new since yesterday's lane. Nov 23 covered the original verification story; today's item is the compression — machine-checked mathematics is now small enough to be maintained, refactored, and adopted by the Lean community like ordinary code. That is the material change.

Buildn / client use. The compression story is the client-facing one: AI-assisted work shrank a 17M-line artifact to 76k lines with the checker still green. That is exactly the "agentic refactor with proof" pitch for gnarly legacy code — same loop, cheaper domain.

  • Same theorems, 154× smaller
  • Independent second-machine check
  • Adopted by Lean community
"Machine-checked only" means the checker agrees with itself across two machines — it does not mean a human understands the argument yet.

Lean + differential testing finds 17 unreported Clojure bugs

try todayopen source
  • s.conao3.com · Nov 2026 · Clojure 1.12.7, JDK 21
  • Highlights: vector-of reduce infinite-loops at 65,505+ elements (0xffe0 mask bug, present since 2010)
  • HAMT collision-node dissoc records the wrong hash
  • HAMT itself clean over 3M differential operations

What it is. An independent developer modelled Clojure core functions as Lean definitions, used bv_decide to search for counterexamples, and reproduced each in real Clojure — 17 bugs absent from JIRA and ask.clojure.org, including an off-by-mask infinite loop in vector-of that has shipped since 2010.

Why it matters now. This is the same week as GRH, but for ordinary software: the method (model one function, prove a property, turn the counterexample into a repro) is directly portable to any codebase with arithmetic at its seams. The mask fix is a two-character diff that a proof validates.

Buildn / client use. A concrete service offer: pick a client's most arithmetic-dense module (billing, scheduling, pagination), model the hot functions in Lean, and ship counterexamples as bug reports. Cheaper than a fuzzing engagement and more definitive.

  • Proof beats fuzzing on edge cases
  • Counterexamples reproduce in prod
  • Works on 15-year-old code
The author notes bv_decide times out beyond ~16 bits on multiply — the method covers small functions, not whole subsystems. Pair it with differential testing for the big structures.

ProofAtlas: open math problems ranked by LLM-assessed importance

worth exploringproduct
  • proofatlas.ai/open-problems · surfaced Nov 25, 2026
  • Top 500 by assessed importance, Top 100 by fame
  • Tracks status notes, dependencies, "useful failures"

What it is. A public atlas that ranks unsolved mathematics problems by LLM-assessed importance, connects claims and dependencies, and records who verified what — an attempt to make the research graph itself machine-navigable.

Why it matters now. After a week where RH, GRH, ω, and Hirsch all moved, a tool for deciding what to point agents at next is the natural complement. The interesting design question is not the ranking but the ledger of failed attempts, which is what multi-agent research actually needs.

Buildn / client use. The "useful failures" ledger is a pattern worth stealing for Buildn OS workflows: agent runs that didn't reach the goal still produce reusable evidence.
