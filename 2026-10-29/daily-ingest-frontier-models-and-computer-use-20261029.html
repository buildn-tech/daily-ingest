Daily Ingest Dossier — Frontier Models + Computer Use

The frontier is shifting from peak benchmark scores toward dependable work completion at a usable price. The practical question is no longer which model is strongest in isolation; it is which model can be assigned a bounded job, expose its uncertainty, and finish inside a cost envelope.

Signal map

Read left to right: capability arrives, becomes operational, then needs a measurement or boundary.

Claude Opus 5.5: long-running work at lower cost

product2026-10-29
  • Source: Anthropic
  • Use: coding, computer use, knowledge work

What it is. Anthropic positions Opus 5.5 as a stronger agentic coding and knowledge-work model, with lower typical workload cost than its predecessor and better resistance to prompt injection and irreversible actions.

Why it matters. Long jobs become more economically plausible when the model spends fewer tokens and makes fewer unsafe detours. The safety claims also point toward model choice as part of the execution boundary.

Buildn/client use. Run a fixed migration or audit packet through Opus 5.5, capture tool traces, retries, cost, and human interventions, then compare against the current default.

Caveat. These are vendor-reported results and the linked page contains benchmark and competitor claims that need independent task-level validation.

GPT-6.1 Terra: computer use becomes a cost decision

product2026-10-29
  • Source: OpenAI
  • Use: forms, visual reasoning, desktop tasks

What it is. OpenAI presents Terra as a faster, cheaper model for high-volume computer-use and visual reasoning tasks such as forms, invoices, technical drawings, and desktop applications.

Why it matters. A cheaper specialist can move routine computer-use out of the premium-model lane, provided the workflow has clear success checks and rollback.

Buildn/client use. Create a two-tier router: Terra for structured visual/desktop tasks, premium reasoning only for exceptions; log outcome quality separately from token cost.

Caveat. Cost and benchmark comparisons are publisher claims; real client economics include browser failures, retries, and review time.

  • Test one bounded workflow
  • Record evidence and cost

Gemini at Work: the universal workplace agent pattern

product2026-10-29
  • Source: Google Cloud
  • Use: knowledge work, media, office automation

What it is. Google describes a single workplace-oriented agent that answers questions, handles knowledge work, creates media, and writes across connected work contexts.

Why it matters. The product direction compresses many narrow assistants into one context-bearing surface, raising the importance of permissions, source boundaries, and clear handoffs.

Buildn/client use. Use it as a reference model for a Buildn intake surface: one request, explicit connected sources, visible evidence, and a final approval step.

Caveat. Universal-agent claims can hide integration and permission complexity; test one narrow job before adopting the surface metaphor.

Capability needs a job contract

practice2026-10-29
  • Source: Buildn editorial synthesis
  • Use: delegation, review, safety

What it is. The common operational unit is a bounded task: inputs, allowed tools, success checks, budget, and a human gate for side effects.

Why it matters. More capable agents increase the value of explicit contracts because they can act farther before asking for help.

Buildn/client use. Turn every recurring agent run into a packet with scope, evidence requirements, stop conditions, and an approval owner.

Caveat. A contract is not a sandbox; permissions and egress controls still need enforcement.
