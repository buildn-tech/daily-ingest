AlloyDB + Valkey memory

fresh signalDurable Agent Infrastructure
  • source: cloud.google.com
  • decision lens: practical leverage

What it is

Google recommends Valkey for hot session memory and AlloyDB AI for durable, hybrid-searchable memory; its cited benchmark reports smaller prompts, lower latency, and lower token cost.

Why it matters

Separate hot context from durable rules and episodes instead of stuffing histories into every prompt.

Buildn / client use

Model Buildn memory as recent state, durable decisions, and evidence-linked artifacts.

Caveat: Benchmarks are workload-dependent; memory extraction and deletion policy remain product work.
  • Next: Define the three memory classes before adding another retrieval layer.

Spanner queues

fresh signalDurable Agent Infrastructure
  • source: cloud.google.com
  • decision lens: practical leverage

What it is

Spanner queues make enqueueing and business-state updates part of one ACID transaction, with scheduled delivery, leases, streaming SQL, and transactional acknowledgements.

Why it matters

The database/queue commit gap is a major source of phantom or lost agent actions.

Buildn / client use

Use the pattern for approval timeouts, handoffs, retries, and artifact publication.

Caveat: Cloud Spanner may be excessive for small systems; idempotent external actions still matter.
  • Next: Write a state-transition table for one approval workflow.

Memory as policy

fresh signalDurable Agent Infrastructure
  • source: cloud.google.com
  • decision lens: practical leverage

What it is

The memory post distinguishes buffer, summary, episodic, and entity/rule memory.

Why it matters

The important design move is policy: what may be recalled, for how long, and with what provenance.

Buildn / client use

Store client constraints and approval decisions as typed, inspectable records.

Caveat: Persistence without consent, deletion, or source attribution becomes a liability.
  • Next: Add source, confidence, retention, and owner fields to memory candidates.
