The AI-Native Services Playbook (Emergence Capital)

read this weekplaybookemcap
  • emcap.com/thoughts/the-ai-native-services-playbook · Spring 2026 edition
  • Core test: is AI doing a material share of the work at high gross margin?
  • Inference costs + human-in-the-loop labor belong in COGS, not opex
  • Updated every six months; next edition Fall 2026

What it is. The first systematic operator guide for selling AI-powered services: team composition (domain credibility is existential), the Mirage PMF trap, pricing and metric design split into leading indicators (is AI actually doing the work?) and lagging ones (is it showing up in the financials?).

Why it matters now. This is exactly the shape of Buildn's offer set — productized service with agents doing delivery work. The "North Star Product Metric = how much of the work AI does" framing is the single most useful number to instrument for Buildn's own engagements.

Buildn / client use. Define the AI-share metric per engagement type (e.g. fraction of deliverables produced end-to-end by agents before human review). Use Mirage PMF as the honest self-audit at each quarterly review; it also doubles as a client-qualification filter — prospects who want "AI" as labor arbitrage will mirror the failure mode.

AI made self-hosting fun again

resonatesessay
  • janlukas.blog · Nov 26, 2026 (HN 640 pts, 329 comments)
  • Agent-assisted infra: half-day bootc image build that would have taken a week
  • Friction, not interest, was what faded

What it is. A first-person account of the agent-era self-hoster: Git-managed infrastructure means agents treat it as a software project, so NixOS, bootc images, and automated deploys went from "someday" to done. The interesting claim is psychological: the enthusiasm didn't return because of AI capability, but because AI removed the friction that rationed it.

Buildn / client use. This is the argument for Buildn's whole positioning in one sentence: the constraint was never competence, it was friction. Steal the framing for client pitches — "your team stopped automating because it was expensive to try; we make trying cheap." Also validates local-first infra ambitions for Env Vault.
