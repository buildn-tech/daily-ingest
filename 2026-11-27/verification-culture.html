The need for a mainstream verifiable programming language

readmanifesto
  • blainehansen.me · Jun 2026, updated Jul 2026 (HN Nov 27, 135 pts)
  • Thesis: LLM acceleration + pre-LLM sloppiness = quality collapse at machine speed
  • Formal verification must reach ordinary environments: browser to embedded
  • De Moura quote: proofs correct ≠ theorem statements capture intent

What it is. The most complete argument for formal verification as the LLM-era engineering response, written from a practitioner's frustration rather than an academic's agenda. Its sharpest point is the human contract: a proof checker guarantees the proof matches the statement, but a human must still confirm the statement is what you meant.

Why it matters now. This week proved the checker-gated pattern is escaping math: the Clojure differential-testing bug hunt showed the loop catches real application bugs. The essay names the missing piece — a mainstream language where verification is the default, not a specialist import.

Buildn / client use. Directly supports the PR-review discipline (checker-first, human reads the theorem statement — in this case the diff). Also a credible talking point for Buildn OS's review-gate design: the gate is cheap precisely because the human reads intent, not line-by-line.

  • Names the human contract
  • Practitioner voice, not hype
  • Aligns with review-gate design

Hard drive pricing has gotten bad

hardwareeconomics
  • blog.ktz.me · Nov 26, 2026 (HN 329 pts)
  • 20TB EasyStore: $249.99 (Nov 2024) → $549.99 (Nov 2026) · $12.50/TB → $27.50/TB
  • Best Buy 8TB special: $299.99 = $37.50/TB — worse per TB than the 20TB
  • 4-drive NAS: $999.96 (2024) → $2,199.96 (2026)

What it is. Eight years of receipts turned into a chart: the long deflation story of storage broke this year, with Black Friday prices up 2.2× on the same SKU in 24 months — the AI data-center demand wave reaching the homelab.

Buildn / client use. Two implications: (1) local-first backup and archive costs are now rising, so size Env Vault's retention policy knowing storage won't save you; (2) consolidation wins — fewer larger disks beat many small ones until prices normalize. Good blog-ammo for any client conversation about "keep everything forever".
