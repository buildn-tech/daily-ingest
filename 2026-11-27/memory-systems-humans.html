Olumino: notes → flashcards with FSRS-7 and Anki round-trip

try todayshow hn
  • olumino.app · HN 104 pts, 45 comments · free for teachers/students
  • FSRS-7 scheduling (open algorithm, KDD '22 lineage)
  • Import Anki decks with review history; export to Anki/Quizlet/Markdown/print
  • Moon-phase memory visual; Revisit queue for cards failed 3×

What it is. A from-scratch spaced-repetition app built on the open FSRS-7 algorithm (the current best-practice scheduler, trained on millions of real reviews), with the two features that decide whether people actually switch: painless Anki import with history, and a one-file full backup. Classes mode adds teacher visibility into who studied and which cards the class keeps failing.

Why it matters now. The Anki ecosystem is aging; FSRS adoption was the proof that scheduling algorithms matter, and Olumino packages the modern scheduler with a modern interaction model (swipe, moons, Revisit queue). The moon visual is a genuinely good progress metaphor — legible at a glance in a way Anki's numbers never were.

Buildn / personal use. Direct for Arabic study: cards read aloud in 16 languages, Japanese-style extras for scripts. Architecturally, the "scheduling as a service over your own cards" pattern maps to agent-memory design — evidence-linked memory (Google's ReasoningBank, covered Nov 15) for machines is the same problem with different retention constants.

  • FSRS-7 out of the box
  • Anki history preserved
  • One-file full export
Young app; the library of shared decks is thin compared to Anki's decades of community content. Start by importing an existing deck, not building new.

An introduction to uncertainty in machine learning

studytutorial
  • chrhenning.com · HN 75 pts · Nov 27
  • Aleatoric vs epistemic uncertainty, calibrated confidence for model outputs

What it is. A focused tutorial on the distinction that matters most when trusting agent outputs: uncertainty the data has (aleatoric) versus uncertainty the model has (epistemic), and why calibrated confidence is a product feature, not a research nicety.

Buildn / client use. Vocabulary for the agent-confidence conversation with clients: when a workflow's confidence is epistemic, the right product response is a human gate (Buildn OS review steps); when it's aleatoric, the right response is more or better data. Nice complement to the week's verification lane.
