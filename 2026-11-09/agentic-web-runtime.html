What: A managed search layer for sites and docs, with indexing and retrieval intended to make content easier for both people and agents to consume.

Why it matters: “Agent-ready content” becomes an operational property—indexing, freshness, citations, and access rules—not a prompt-writing exercise.

Buildn/client use: Offer a small agent-discovery audit: crawl docs, test retrieval tasks, score provenance, then expose only high-confidence surfaces.

Caveat: search quality and permissions can diverge; test unauthorized retrieval explicitly.
  • Index the real knowledge surface.
  • Measure answer provenance.
  • Keep permissions first-class.

Buildn/client use: Benchmark a disposable job runner on cold start, policy enforcement, artifact persistence, and reviewer handoff.

Caveat: preview convenience can hide durable side effects; define the commit boundary.
  • Benchmark reset time.
  • Capture artifacts by default.
  • Separate preview from publish.

The Agent Access Model

What: A model for granting agents scoped access rather than treating them as human users with copied credentials.

Why it matters: As agents cross product boundaries, access terms need to be legible to operators and enforceable at request time.

Buildn/client use: Make every job manifest declare owner, purpose, tools, data scope, expiration, and escalation path.

  • Bind access to purpose.
  • Make expiry normal.
  • Log delegation context.

Trusted delegation and personal-agent visibility

What: Cloudflare’s adjacent work frames agents as principals that need explicit delegation and user-facing visibility into what they can see and do.

Why it matters: Consent is not a one-time OAuth screen when an agent acts asynchronously or chains tools.

Buildn/client use: Prototype a “why this action?” panel: initiating human, delegated scope, tool call, data touched, and undo/rollback.

  • Show the chain of authority.
  • Expose the next action.
  • Design revocation early.
