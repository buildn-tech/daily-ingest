Daily Ingest Dossier — Evaluation Radar

20 September 2026 · adaptive lane

Evaluation is moving from static leaderboards toward living catalogs, deployment-aware reliability, and persistent system behavior. The useful move for Buildn is to make evidence provenance and cross-runtime replay part of delivery—not a final report.

Signal map

Benchmark Radar

living database

What: Searchable catalog joining benchmark papers, repositories, datasets, model reports, score histories, and provenance.

Why it matters: It reduces the cost of finding a relevant test while preserving source-specific records instead of flattening incomparable scores.

Use: Seed Buildn’s evaluation library and audit whether a proposed metric duplicates existing work.

Caveat: Catalog coverage and source quality still require review.

Action: Browse its agentic/tool-use records and select three reusable tests.

DAREBench

reliability

What: Deployment-aware evaluation framing for models and agents in operational settings.

Why it matters: A high task score is weak evidence if the system fails under latency, tool, environment, or deployment constraints.

Use: Pair capability scores with timeout, recovery, cost, and artifact-integrity measures.

Caveat: Research benchmarks may not map directly to client workloads.

Action: Add deployment conditions to the next internal eval brief.

Persistent identity benchmark

memory

What: Benchmark for persistent identity in deployed AI agents, separating continuity from simple recall.

Why it matters: Long-running assistants need stable self/role boundaries, not only retrieval accuracy.

Use: Test whether Buildn agents preserve workspace identity, permissions, and user preferences safely.

Caveat: Identity criteria can be domain-specific and normative.

Action: Define allowed, required, and forbidden continuity across sessions.

The Unreliable Progress Bar

operator UX

What: Empirical challenge to trusting agent-authored progress reports.

Why it matters: Visibility is only useful when claims can be checked against execution evidence.

Use: Build milestone-based status from tool traces, tests, and outputs.

Caveat: Not all progress is observable without instrumentation.

Action: Replace “70% complete” with the next verifiable gate.

Mapping benchmark design

research

What: Study of what benchmarks measure and how design choices shape reported conclusions.

Why it matters: Benchmark selection encodes a product’s definition of competence.

Use: Document task distribution, graders, uncertainty, and failure costs before comparing agents.

Caveat: Broad meta-analysis is not a substitute for task-level validation.

Action: Add a one-page “what this eval cannot tell us” note.
