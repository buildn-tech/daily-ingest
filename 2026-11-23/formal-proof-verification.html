Daily Ingest Dossier — Formal Proof Verification at Scale

Daily Ingest Dossier · 2026-11-23

A machine-verified proof of the Riemann Hypothesis landed this week, and the verification story, not the mathematics, is the transferable pattern: independent recomputation, isolated claims, and immutability at 17M-line scale.

Lean verification of the Riemann Hypothesis

LandmarkResearch · Verification
  • Proof released: 2026-11-20 (Levent Alpöge)
  • Library size: 17,000,000+ lines of Lean
  • Depends only on Mathlib; statement already vetted in Mathlib

What it is. The Lean and Mathlib communities jointly certified a 17M-line machine-checked proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, released November 20. Verification hinged on three independent checks: reproducible compilation, an isolated Mathlib-only statement file compared with two independent tools, and execution of the proof kernel on an inert export immune to any malicious code in the proof sources.

Why it matters now. This is the strongest existing demonstration of evidence-bearing artifacts at the limit of scale: the trust story is not "believe the author" but "recompile, compare, and execute the checker yourself". That is exactly the review model agent-generated code is converging toward.

Buildn/client use. The pattern maps directly onto reviewable agent delivery: isolate the claim (statement file), verify by independent recomputation, and keep the artifact immutable for audit. Worth mirroring in Buildn's review workflow design.

Caveats. Verifying correctness is not understanding; the mathematics still needs the community to digest it.

Action. Read the certification post and note the four acceptance criteria; they are a ready-made checklist for "what counts as verified" in agent workflows.

What it is. An engineering-level look at what a 17M-line proof library actually costs: compile time, memory, CI, and the practical limits of anyone reproducing it.

Why it matters. Scale breaks naive verification even when the logic is sound; infrastructure (caching, distribution, comparators) becomes the bottleneck, not proof theory.

Use. A caution for Buildn's artifact system: big evidence artifacts need explicit distribution and verification-cost budgets.
