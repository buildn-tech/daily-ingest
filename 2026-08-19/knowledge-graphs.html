Spanner Graph + Data Commons: public meets private

GAGraphRAGprivate instance
  • Updated: 2026-08-19 (original US post: 2026-08-04)
  • Scale cited: 1.7B nodes · 2.6B edges · 400B+ observations

Google describes Data Commons on Spanner Graph as a way to connect public datasets with private organizational data, using GQL, incremental updates, consistent snapshots, and natural-language GraphRAG.

  • Model relationships instead of copies
  • Keep private data access-controlled
  • Traverse multi-hop context dynamically
  • Preserve snapshot consistency

Buildn/client use: link project, client, artifact, decision, and evidence entities so an agent can answer “why” with traceable paths.

Caveat: graph quality depends on entity resolution, permissions, and source freshness; GraphRAG does not remove those costs.

Action: model one client engagement as a five-node graph and test three multi-hop questions.

Cortex Framework v7: enterprise data products for agents

GASAPDataform

Google’s v7 turns SAP ERP and SAP Business Data Cloud data into semantic, reusable products deployed through BigQuery and registered in Knowledge Catalog. It separates standard content from customer extensions and supports incremental processing, CDC, and natural-language data-product building.

  • Business terms beat raw field names
  • Extensions survive standard updates
  • Incremental pipelines reduce rebuilds

Use now: copy the separation principle: keep Buildn’s canonical artifact schema stable, and isolate client-specific fields and transformations.

Caveat: this is strongest for enterprise data estates; a small workspace should not inherit SAP-scale machinery without a concrete retrieval failure.

Action: write a “semantic contract” for one artifact type: fields, owner, freshness, permissions, and example questions.

APQC 2026 KM priorities: AI needs operating ownership

research baselineknowledge management
  • Published: 2026-01-07
  • Publisher: APQC

APQC’s survey frames KM priorities around maturity, AI integration, scale, threats, and future-ready skills. The useful implication is operational: AI adoption makes ownership, taxonomy, and knowledge-loss controls more important, not less.

  • Track knowledge freshness explicitly
  • Assign owners to critical concepts
  • Measure reuse, not document count

Use now: add a “knowledge health” view to projects: stale facts, orphaned decisions, missing evidence, and last successful reuse.

Action: read the survey report and extract three measurable KM indicators.
