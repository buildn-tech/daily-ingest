2026-11-20 · lane selected for fresh evidence on measuring, scaling, and governing agent-assisted research

The newest signal is operational, not theatrical: agents are now capable of multi-hour research work, but performance is uneven and spend is rising fast. The useful design question is where to insert human judgment, how to choose coordination patterns, and how to measure output without confusing activity for progress.

OpenAI reports large gains on some multi-hour tasks, but sharp variation by work type: monitoring training runs is far easier than launching them or writing serving-stack code.

Buildn/client use: Split discovery, implementation, and production changes into separately scored work units.

Why now: Agent workdays and spending are scaling faster than simple success metrics.

Caveat: self-reported internal data; output volume is not proof of research progress.
  • Define task-level acceptance tests
  • Track intervention rate
  • Price work by outcome

Choose coordination by task shape

evidence-ledagent architecture
  • Google Research · 180 configurations
  • Best architecture predicted: 87%
  • Centralized finance gain: +80.9%

Google’s study finds multi-agent systems help when work is parallelizable and decomposable; sequential dependencies make coordination overhead and error propagation liabilities.

Buildn/client use: Add a “decomposability check” before proposing multi-agent orchestration.

Why now: A cheap single agent often beats a busy swarm on tightly coupled work.

Caveat: benchmark results depend on task families and model mix.
  • Map dependency depth first
  • Use parallel lanes selectively
  • Measure communication overhead

Mars as a bounded-autonomy pattern

validated workflowphysical operations
  • NASA JPL + Anthropic
  • ~400m route · 500k+ variables simulated
  • Human engineers approved commands

Claude Code planned a Perseverance route from mission context and imagery, generated XML rover commands, and passed the plan through the existing high-fidelity simulation before transmission.

Buildn/client use: Use the pattern for high-consequence automation: generate, simulate, inspect, approve, execute.

Why now: It demonstrates that autonomy expands when the validation boundary is explicit.

Caveat: this is assisted planning, not unsupervised rover control.
  • Keep execution gates external
  • Simulate before side effects
  • Log every approval
