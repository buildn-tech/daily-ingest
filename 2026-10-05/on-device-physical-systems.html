On-Device Intelligence + Physical Systems

Two different-looking launches share one practical thesis: intelligence is moving closer to the operating surface. Googlebook packages local, proactive assistance into a personal computer, while Project Suncatcher tests whether compute infrastructure itself can move into orbit. The near-term Buildn opportunity is to design workflows that degrade gracefully when cloud access is slow, costly, or unavailable.

Signal map

  • Local context can stay local.
  • Infrastructure becomes a design variable.
  • Offline behavior needs intent.

Googlebook: intelligence in the interaction loop

available nowhardware

Googlebook’s Built-In Intelligence

What it is: A laptop with context-aware pointer actions, structured dictation, custom widgets, Linux tooling, and background AI task processing.

Why it matters: The unit of product design becomes a summoned capability in the current workspace, not a separate chatbot tab.

Buildn/client use: Design “bring intelligence to the artifact” flows: select a brief, repo, or meeting note, then ask for a bounded transformation with explicit confirmation.

Caveat: Proactive/background behavior raises privacy, permissions, and interruption-design questions; the hardware is regionally rolling out.

Next: Prototype one context-summoned action with a visible preview and undo.

Project Suncatcher: compute as a physical system

long horizonresearch

Google: Project Suncatcher prototype is in orbit

What it is: A prototype satellite launched with Planet to test TPU behavior under radiation, thermal extremes, and spaceflight stress.

Why it matters: It turns compute location, energy, thermal design, and network delay into first-class AI infrastructure constraints.

Buildn/client use: Use the frame for edge/remote deployments: specify which inference must be local, what can queue, and which artifacts must remain available during disconnection.

Caveat: This is a research moonshot, not a deployable hosting option.

Next: Add a “degraded mode” column to one workflow architecture and define its minimum useful output.

Physical AI benefits from concrete curricula

field signalrobotics

FIRST Global Challenge 2026 · FIRST CANOPY 2026–27

What it is: International robotics programs organized around wildfire response and biodiversity, with real game manuals, CAD assets, teams, and mentorship.

Why it matters: Physical AI becomes legible when the task, environment, scoring, and collaboration model are explicit.

Buildn/client use: Borrow the pattern for workshops and client discovery: define a constrained environment, measurable objective, shared artifacts, and post-run review.

Caveat: Education competition artifacts are not production robotics specifications.

Next: Use one challenge brief as a template for a 90-minute agent workflow design exercise.
