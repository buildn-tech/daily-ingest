CRA readiness as a journey

practical guidepolicy

OpenSSF: Grow CRA Readiness

What it is: A role-based “community garden” path through EU Cyber Resilience Act responsibilities and resources.

Why it matters: The same project can be an upstream maintainer, a steward, or an ingredient inside a marketed product; responsibilities differ materially.

Buildn/client use: Create a one-page dependency intake that asks role, distribution, support posture, vulnerability process, and evidence owner.

Caveat: It is guidance, not legal advice; applicability depends on the actual product and organizational role.

Next: Run the journey against one Buildn dependency and save the missing-evidence list as tickets.

Participation is an operating capability

strategicopen source

OpenSSF case study: IBM and open-source participation

What it is: An enterprise case connecting open-source participation with platform strategy, security learning, customer support, and employee development.

Why it matters: Production dependence creates obligations beyond “we use the package”; contribution and governance are risk-management tools.

Buildn/client use: Map the top five critical dependencies to maintainers, release cadence, security contacts, and contribution options.

Caveat: Large-enterprise investment does not translate directly to a small team’s budget.

Next: Pick one dependency where a small documentation, test, or triage contribution buys disproportionate trust.

Evidence beats a compliance binder

workflowdelivery

Editorial read: The reusable pattern is a lightweight evidence bundle attached to delivery: owner, version, source, vulnerability path, support expectation, and decision.

  • Evidence travels with releases.
  • Ownership is explicit.
  • Exceptions become visible.
