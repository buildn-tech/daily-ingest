Quad9 pulls out of France rather than poison DNS

internet governancefront page
  • Effective Nov 24, 2026 · announced Oct 22
  • French court (beIN suit): daily fines for refusing to block domains
  • Quad9 chose withdrawal over court-ordered domain blocking

What it is. After a French court imposed escalating fines (cut from €580k to €1,000/day) over DNS blocking for alleged copyright infringement, Quad9 deactivated its service for French users on Nov 24 rather than selectively poison resolution.

Why it matters now. A public-interest resolver was effectively forced out of a market because it was reputable enough to be sued. Cloudflare's "canary in the coal mine" post frames the precedent: transparency invites liability. Anyone running privacy infrastructure in the EU should read this as a compliance-planning input.

Buildn / client use. Reminder for self-hosting recommendations: choice of DNS/security services is a jurisdictional decision. Clients serving EU users need a fallback resolver story.

  • Principled withdrawal over compliance
  • Precedent targets reputable operators
  • EU DNS strategy needs a plan B

Active intrusion campaign vs. Check Point management servers

act nowsecurity
  • greynoise.io · published Nov 24, 2026 · 282 points on HN
  • Target: security management servers (the highest-value control plane)

What it is. GreyNoise documents an active campaign against Check Point security management servers — the consoles that manage the firewalls, i.e. compromising one means compromising the fleet.

Why it matters now. Control-plane targeting keeps proving out: attackers go for the management layer, not the endpoint. Any client with security appliances should verify management-interface exposure today.

Buildn / client use. Immediate checklist item for client security reviews: management planes isolated from the internet, patched, and MFA'd. Also the recurring argument for Env Vault's "control plane is the asset" threat model.

  • Management plane is the target
  • Check exposure today
  • One console = whole fleet

Wakeshift: MCP Events — let a server wake your agent

try todayopen source
  • github.com/wakeshift/server · Go/TypeScript · Show HN Nov 24
  • Implements MCP Events per spec; also wakes agents that can't subscribe yet
  • Self-hostable or hosted

What it is. The missing half of MCP for asynchronous work: servers can push events to agents, so an agent doesn't have to poll or stay resident. Falls back to waking agents that lack native subscription support.

Why it matters now. Event-driven agent execution keeps earning inclusion because polling is the worst of both worlds — slow and expensive. A spec-track events layer makes Buildn's "agent responds when the world changes" workflows practical instead of hacky.

Buildn / client use. Direct candidate for Hermes agent infrastructure: wake agents on webhook events (email arrived, PR merged, alert fired) without a resident process. Very young project — track, don't depend yet.

  • Agents become event-driven, not polled
  • Spec-track, not a vendor lock
  • Self-hostable fallback included
Four commits, no releases yet — prototype-grade. Watch the spec alignment more than the code.

PQCrypta Proxy: post-quantum TLS at the edge, self-hosted

worth exploringopen source
  • github.com/PQCrypta/pqcrypta-proxy · Rust · active Nov 25
  • HTTP/3, QUIC, hybrid X25519MLKEM768 TLS, JA3/JA4 fingerprinting, Prometheus, hot reload

What it is. A Rust layer-7 reverse proxy with hybrid post-quantum TLS and modern edge features (fingerprinting, geo-blocking, circuit breaking, ML-adaptive rate limiting).

Why it matters now. PQC is moving from whitepaper to default proxy config. A self-hosted option means regulated clients can adopt hybrid key exchange without a CDN migration.

Buildn / client use. Reference for client edge hardening where Caddy/Nginx PQC support lags; also useful as a spec checklist for what modern proxies are expected to do.
